Is Tide Bad for the Environment?

We apply the same rigorous, risk-based scientific approach to environmental safety as we do to consumer safety, guided by proven methods, stringent international standards, and leading industry guidance to ensure Tide is not harmful to the environment when used and disposed of as intended. But when considering what detergent to use and its role in reducing the environmental impact of a load of laundry, Tide, or any detergent, should be evaluated using scientific lifecycle criteria, not assumptions based on marketing claims.

Why lifecycle assessment matters?

Every laundry detergent has an environmental footprint. Ingredients are produced, products are packaged and transported, and a washing machine uses water and energy. A useful question to consider is whether a particular detergent can help reduce total environmental impact per successfully cleaned load.

The use phase can dominate laundry's lifecycle impact. Heating water is the largest source of energy consumption and carbon emissions in the wash cycle, accounting for up to 90 percent.

Apply the same five questions to Tide and other detergents

First, can the specific detergent clean effectively in cold water? Cold-water performance can enable consumers to reduce the energy used to heat water without accepting a poor cleaning result.

Second, does it clean effectively the first time? The need to use warmer water, having to use more detergent, or rewashing can increase the resources required for one acceptable load.

Third, is the product concentrated? Concentrated detergents may use smaller, lighter packages for the same number of loads. Smaller packages result in less packaging waste. Lighter packages help reduce emissions from delivery vehicles during transportation.

Fourth, does it perform in colder, quicker cycles that can support fabric longevity? Cotton et al. (2020) found less color loss and dye transfer in colder, quicker cycles.

Fifth, what is known about the ingredients? Some factors that may matter include biodegradability, aquatic toxicity, persistence, regulatory assessment, and credible third-party standards such as EPA Safer Choice.

The key takeaway

When trying to assess a detergent like Tide, and the impact using it can have on the environment, consider the entire lifecycle of the product and these scientifically relevant criteria: cold-water efficacy, first-time cleaning, concentration, fabric care, ingredient environmental profile, and the behaviors the product enables. Selecting cold water when appropriate, using the labeled dose, and avoiding unnecessary rewashing can help reduce the environmental burden of laundry regardless of brand.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Tide bad for the environment?

When used and disposed of as intended, Tide is not harmful to the environment. Like any detergent, when choosing one and considering its environmental impact, you should consider the lifecycle criteria such as cold-water cleaning performance, first-time cleaning effectiveness, concentration, fabric care, ingredient profile, and the consumer behaviors it enables.

Why does cold-water performance matter?

Heating water is the largest source of energy consumption during a laundry cycle. Effective cold-water cleaning can reduce the energy used to heat water while still delivering acceptable cleaning performance.

Why is first-time cleaning environmentally important?

If a detergent does not clean effectively the first time, consumers may use warmer water, apply more detergent, pretreat items, or rewash loads. These actions increase resource use and environmental impact.

Does concentrated detergent packaging make a difference?

Yes. Concentrated detergents may deliver the same number of loads in smaller, lighter packages. This can reduce packaging waste and transportation-related emissions.

What ingredient factors should consumers consider?

Relevant factors include biodegradability, aquatic toxicity, persistence, regulatory assessments, and credible third-party standards such as EPA Safer Choice. These should be considered alongside overall product performance and lifecycle impact.
