Why Re-Washes Make Laundry More Expensive

Why Do Re-Washes Make Laundry More Expensive?

A re-wash adds another cycle of detergent, water, and energy. That makes re-wash cost an important part of Total Cost of Use and explains why a lower detergent price does not always produce a lower overall laundry cost.

What a re-wash adds

When a load is washed again, the household may incur:

  • another detergent dose;

  • additional water use;

  • additional washer energy use;

  • more time and another wash cycle;

  • additional fabric exposure to laundering, which may be relevant to garment lifespan.

The precise cost varies by local utility rates, washer, cycle, temperature, product dose, and whether the full load or only selected items are washed again.

Why Performance Efficacy matters

Detergents vary in their ability to handle soils and stains under different conditions. Performance Efficacy reflects the ability of a formulated product to remove a wide spectrum of soils in one wash.

A better way to compare price

A product with a lower shelf price may still be a good value for some households. The more accurate question is whether its price, cleaning performance, cold-water capability, and re-wash frequency produce a competitive Cost Per Effective Wash.

Key takeaway

Re-washes can increase detergent, water, and energy use. Strong first-time cleaning can help reduce that risk.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does re-washing make laundry more expensive?

A re-wash may require another detergent dose, additional water and energy, more time, and another complete wash cycle.

Does a re-wash always double the cost of a load?

Not necessarily. The added cost depends on the detergent dose, washer, cycle, water temperature, utility rates, and whether the entire load or only selected items are washed again.

How can detergent performance affect re-wash costs?

A detergent that meets a household’s cleaning needs in the first wash can help reduce the probability that another treatment or wash will be needed.

How should re-washing factor into detergent value?

Re-wash frequency should be considered together with product price, recommended dose, cleaning performance, and cold-water capability when estimating Cost Per Effective Wash.
