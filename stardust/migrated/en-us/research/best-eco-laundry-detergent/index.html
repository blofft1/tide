What Is the Best Eco-Friendly Laundry Detergent?

If you’re searching for a detergent to help you reduce the impact of a load of laundry, the best choice is a detergent that delivers the lowest credible environmental impact per successfully cleaned load, not simply the product with the best marketing.

Look at the outcome, not just the label

Terms such as “eco-friendly”, “green”, “natural”, “plant-based”, “plastic-free”, and “minimalist” can describe selected product attributes or brand positioning. They do not, by themselves, confirm that a detergent delivers a lower overall lifecycle impact. Laundry is an interacting system: detergent performance, wash temperature, dose, packaging, transportation, fabric care, ingredient environmental profiles, and consumer behavior all influence the impact a load can have on the environment.

A scientifically useful comparison therefore focuses on environmental impact per successfully cleaned load. A product can appear favorable per dose, but the conclusion may change if acceptable cleaning requires warmer water, extra detergent, pretreatment, or a second wash.

Five criteria for comparing detergents

  1. Cold-water cleaning efficacy. Heating wash water is the dominant energy demand in the laundry wash cycle. Multiple sources confirm that 80-90% of the energy used in a laundry cycle is for heating the water.

  2. Prevention of compensatory behaviors. Underperformance can prompt warmer washes, larger doses, pretreatment, or rewashing. A 2024 study observed compensatory behavior among users of a lower-performing eco-brand detergent.

  3. Concentration. Concentrated powders, liquids, pods, sheets, and tiles can deliver more loads in smaller, lighter packages, which can help reduce packaging material and transportation-related emissions.

  4. Support for fabric longevity. Colder, quicker cycles have been shown to cause less color loss and dye transfer and may help garments remain usable longer (Cotton et al., 2020).

  5. Ingredient environmental profile. Biodegradability, aquatic toxicity, persistence, and regulatory assessments are relevant. EPA Safer Choice is one example of a third-party credential that you can use in evaluating ingredients.

What to look for

Look for credible evidence that the detergent works in cold water and follow label dosing directions for load size and soil level. Consider whether the format is concentrated, whether the product has relevant third-party ingredient credentials, and whether the cleaning result is likely to avoid rewashing.

Low-temperature-active enzymes and advanced surfactants can support cold-water cleaning, but also verify the performance of a detergent from independent testing results and your own experience. Also keep in mind that biodegradability of a product or its ingredients can be an important product attribute, but it is not a complete sustainability score.

The bottom line

There is no scientifically responsible shortcut based on one front-of-pack claim. Compare products on the same lifecycle-based criteria and prioritize successful cleaning in cold water. The strongest choice is the one that helps deliver the required clean while reducing resources across the full laundry process.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes a laundry detergent eco-friendly?

A detergent should be evaluated by its environmental impact per successfully cleaned load rather than by a single claim such as “green,” “natural,” “plant-based,” or “plastic-free.” Relevant factors include cleaning performance, wash temperature, dosage, packaging, transportation, fabric care, ingredient environmental profiles, and the potential need for pretreatment or rewashing.

Is cold-water cleaning important when choosing an eco laundry detergent?

Yes. Heating wash water accounts for a large share of the energy used during a laundry cycle. A detergent that cleans effectively in cold water can help reduce the energy required for a successfully cleaned load.

Are concentrated detergents better for the environment?

Concentrated detergents can provide more loads in smaller, lighter packages, which can help reduce packaging material and transportation-related emissions. Concentration should still be considered alongside cleaning effectiveness and the other stages of the laundry lifecycle.

Does biodegradable automatically mean environmentally friendly?

No. Biodegradability is an important product or ingredient attribute, but it is not a complete sustainability score. Aquatic toxicity, persistence, regulatory assessments, cold-water performance, packaging, dosage, fabric care, and the avoidance of rewashing may also be relevant.

How can I compare eco laundry detergents?

Look for credible cold-water performance, follow label dosing directions, consider concentration and relevant third-party ingredient credentials, and assess whether the detergent is likely to clean successfully without requiring a second wash.
